The Three-Era Data Regime — Your Strongest Data-Awareness Contribution

The corpus is experimental sciences, 2008–2026, both sessions per year (موضوع 1 principale + موضوع 2 استثنائية) with solutions — roughly 13 years × 2 sessions ≈ 26 exam instances per subject. But those years are not exchangeable: the Algerian Ministry applied عتبة الدروس (an official curriculum-reduction "threshold") during the COVID period, administratively removing large parts of the programme from exam scope. Treating 2020–2022 as normal years silently corrupts every frequency and forecasting model. This section defines the correction — and it is a genuine methodological contribution the supervisors' proposal does not anticipate.

The three treatments, made concrete

  1. Regime field — every observation carries exam_regime ∈ {golden, pandemic_reduced, modern} (set in P4's corpus_meta.json).
  2. Censoring rule — cut topics in pandemic years are missing/censored, not zero (P8 series construction). This is the core fix that prevents a false "this topic is dying" trend.
  3. Recency weighting — the GBM branch takes sample_weight increasing toward recent years; regime becomes a model feature (P8/P9).
The honest split, and one hard rule — never backtest on pandemic years. Their distorted frequencies would reward or punish models for the wrong reasons.
  • Train pool: 2008–2022 (golden + pandemic, pandemic censored & down-weighted)
  • Backtest / model comparison: 2023, 2024, 2025 — all modern, where the models must actually perform (this is where the grand comparison + CIs live)
  • Sealed real-world holdout: 2026 — predicted once, at the very end, by the winning model only. "Trained on 2008–2025, our system predicted the June 2026 BAC topics at X%" is your freshest, most defensible, most demo-able result.
Why this matters in the viva: this single design choice shows you understand your data's real-world generating process — administrative curriculum policy — not just its numbers. It also splits your model families honestly: the GBM/feature branch absorbs censoring + weighting cleanly, while ARIMA/Prophet/LSTM/Transformer cannot gracefully swallow a censored gap and must be fit on non-cut topics or the modern segment. That asymmetry is itself a Chapter-6 finding ("time-series models degrade under administrative structural breaks; feature-based models absorb them"), and it further explains why the GBM/hybrid tends to win under this regime.
